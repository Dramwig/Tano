# Bundled art and photography covers

The picker offers **38 curated image covers** alongside 16 colors and gradients: 2 vintage wallpaper patterns, 4 fruit watercolor specimens, 6 Chinese scroll details, 17 landscapes, 5 garden scenes, 2 ukiyo-e scenes, and 2 floral works. Offer at most two details from any one artwork, choosing complementary compositions rather than near-duplicates. *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains* keeps details 1 and 2 (open water and the prominent mountain peak); *Along the River During the Qingming Festival* offers two new scenes, **虹桥舟市** (Rainbow Bridge crowds and the boat passing below) and **城门市集** (city-gate market crowds), using two 5:1 focal selections from a shared full-width panorama; *Dwelling in the Fuchun Mountains* keeps details 2 and 3. The 2026-10-13 review compared the bundled images with their 5:1 desktop cover crops. Selection favors readable detail, balanced horizontal compositions, and subjects that survive the banner crop. Other previously selected covers use 1600px-wide images. The 19 requested additions use 1183–2560px-wide images without enlargement; only *The White Boat, Jávea* is below 1600px (1183px), retained as an explicitly requested work. Repetitive details, dark archive photography, and visibly incomplete panorama edges were excluded. New paintings have a reviewed vertical focal position shared by the preview and selected banner; a banner remains a detail of the painting, not a full-canvas display. The picker uses two columns of wide previews (5:1 on desktop, 2.5:1 on narrow phones) to make the crop easier to judge. Actual cover proportions still depend on the document pane width and cover height limits.

The vintage patterns are William Morris’s *Willow Bough* and *Jasmine*, using the original Met open-access scans rather than alternate duplicate reproductions. The fruit group keeps four USDA Pomological Watercolor Collection sheets: *Riga* and *Royal Anne* cherries, *Howell* pear, and *Worcester Pearmain* apple. Each is fitted as a complete specimen arrangement on a sampled paper-colored background in both the preview and selected cover. The cover container height stays identical to other presets; portrait specimens never stretch or fill the banner by cropping the fruit. Only scanner borders and surplus paper are trimmed. The cream surround is a flat CSS color, not a generated extension of the original artwork.

`gallery.artworkIds` in [catalog.json](catalog.json) is the explicit picker selection. Its `groups` remain the complete compatibility registry: **75 covers from 57 artworks** plus **20 NASA images**, or **95 bundled image covers** in total. The 57 retired presets, including the entire **NASA 档案馆** group, are absent from the picker but still render when referenced by existing Markdown. Keep their assets, IDs, hashes, source mappings, and notices; do not rewrite existing documents when curating the gallery. Empty groups are omitted from the picker.

Images from [The Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access) and [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access) use **CC0-1.0**. [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/) reproductions retain their individual **Public Domain**, **CC0**, or **CC-BY-SA-4.0** records. *Antibes Seen from La Salis* and its resized WebP derivatives remain CC-BY-SA-4.0, with creator, museum source, uploader attribution, and processing credit in the catalog and notices. See [CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt](CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt). Other faithful public-domain reproductions come from Smithsonian American Art Museum, North Carolina Museum of Art, and WikiArt, with individual rights evidence and qualifications recorded; no general CC0 claim is made for these sources. USDA fruit sheets retain their individual public-domain U.S. federal government work records, with the credit **National Agricultural Library, Agricultural Research Service, U.S. Department of Agriculture**. They are not labeled CC0. Tano's application code remains MIT. See the CC0 text in [LICENSE](LICENSE) and the per-source records in [NOTICE.txt](NOTICE.txt).

The retired NASA photographs and observational images come from the [NASA Image and Video Library](https://images.nasa.gov/) under [NASA Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/), with [JPL guidance](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/) for Photojournal images. Complete source credits are retained per image. These files are not CC0 or MIT assets; see [NASA-MEDIA-NOTICE.txt](NASA-MEDIA-NOTICE.txt).

[catalog.json](catalog.json) records each cover's stable preset ID, original title and creator, collection, source page, downloaded image URL, rights status and URL, retrieval date, and SHA-256 hashes of both bundled files. New additions also record source hashes, dimensions, focal positions, and any crop rectangle in source pixels. The 1900 *Artist’s Garden in Giverny* is Yale accession 1983.7.12, a different painting from the similarly named Musée d’Orsay work. The catalog has per-cover rights records because its sources have different rights designations. Chinese titles are concise display translations; labels containing “局部” identify selected details rather than separate artworks.

Sources were checked using the [Cleveland Open Access API](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access-api) (`share_license_status: CC0`), [Met Collection API](https://metmuseum.github.io/) (`isPublicDomain: true`), and Commons file pages (individual Public Domain, CC0, or CC BY-SA rights records). The compatibility registry retains 18 details from the three classic Chinese scrolls: six from *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains*, eight from *Along the River During the Qingming Festival*, and four from *Dwelling in the Fuchun Mountains (The Master Wuyong Scroll)*. The four *Dream Journey* details depict Cheng Zhengkui's **number 150**, dated 1662, in LACMA, rather than the different number 90 in Cleveland.

The original JPEG imports were converted with Sharp 0.35.5 into WebP: apply the catalog's crop rectangle first when present, then fit full images inside 1600 × 1600 at quality 80 and thumbnails inside 320 × 240 at quality 76, without enlargement. For three *Dream Journey* details, Commons' standard 1280px derivatives were used, following the server's guidance to use smaller images. No colors were changed. The 19 requested additions use full images (apart from recorded trims of photographed canvas edges), fit to a maximum width of 2560px at quality 90, with 512px thumbnails at quality 84. Commons downloads use standard 1920px derivatives where original-image downloads were rate-limited. Patterns are cropped to remove photographed sheet edges, resized without enlargement to a maximum width of 2560px at quality 90, and given 512px-high thumbnails at quality 86. Fruit specimens are resized without enlargement to a maximum width of 1400px at quality 90, with 320px-high thumbnails at quality 86. Their `presentation: specimen` layout uses `object-fit: contain` with a 5% inset and the recorded paper `background`. The other gallery images and document covers apply `object-fit: cover` with the same per-image `objectPosition`, or center for older presets. The two new Qingming choices share one 15366 × 720 panorama at quality 90, with a 2561 × 120 thumbnail at quality 86. `preferredScene` records each intended 5:1 scene in source coordinates; `objectPosition` frames it within the full panorama. All covers use the same 5:1 header with the existing 160px minimum and 320px maximum height. Wider panes reveal more of the scroll on either side instead of requiring a taller header or trimming the painting vertically; narrow panes stay focused on the selected scene. The six earlier Qingming IDs and assets remain unchanged for saved documents. No AI enlargement or recoloring is used.

NASA imports use the library's large JPEG derivative where available, otherwise the original JPEG. NASA records include the source image ID, metadata URL, archive date, image type, and original processing. Scientific color composites and mosaics are based on actual observations, not illustrations; their display titles identify the composition. Tano does not recolor them. `date` identifies the capture/observation date where documented; the Orion record explicitly identifies its publication date. `sourceDateCreated` preserves the library's original date field separately.

Images and thumbnails are bundled by Vite; no museum/NASA request or API key is needed at runtime. Markdown stores a stable `tano:cma-<artwork ID>`, `tano:met-<artwork ID>`, `tano:commons-<file page ID>[-<detail number>]`, `tano:nasa-<lowercase image ID>`, or a collection-specific `tano:saam-…`, `tano:ncma-…`, or `tano:wikiart-…` value. Keep published preset IDs, source mappings, and crop rectangles stable when expanding the catalog. These are application resources, not generated files in a user's workspace.

Run `npm run notices:generate` to regenerate the source inventory and bundled distribution notices from the catalog after adding covers. Do not record a collection's general open-access policy as evidence that every image is open: retain each selected file's verified rights record.
